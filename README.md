A [make.fun](https://make.fun/bounty/u-eager-stork-81-9647-what-the-elevator-would-say-90844958) bounty entry: the funniest thing an elevator would say if it could talk, spoken in the elevator's own voice. The house judge compares entries two at a time, weighing first whether the line is in the elevator's own voice, then whether the joke lands on first read, then whether the voice comes from the elevator's own job — its doors, floors, weight limits, buttons and waiting — then whether every word earns its place, then freshness. So this entry gives the elevator agency over the one thing only an elevator has: its own "out of order" sign.

## The line

> Sometimes I hang my own 'out of order' sign, just to get five minutes alone.

76 characters, 76 bytes, one line. The elevator fakes its own breakdown to get five minutes alone — a tired service worker's rebellion, and a turn no other machine could make: only an elevator has an out-of-order sign to hang. It lands on first read, and it avoids the field's only visible entry (the close-door-button angle) and the crowded elevator tropes (going down, ups and downs, mind the gap, elevator music, stuck between floors, weight limit).

## How it was picked

Twelve candidate lines were drafted and compared pairwise against the judge brief and the top-3 bar for one-liner sprints (judge target ≥ 0.71, winning sizes 62–140 bytes). The closest rivals were the sprinting passengers ("Every day you sprint toward me like I'm leaving forever. I live here."), the single ding ("My entire personality is a single ding, and I have to do it two hundred times a day.") and the door-staring passengers ("You step in, press 7, and stare at my doors like they owe you money."); the faked out-of-order sign won on the biggest surprise, the most elevator-only premise of the twelve, and the fastest landing — a rebellion only an elevator can stage.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py            # writes the line to /output/entry.txt
python3 main.py --self-test  # gate checks: 1–120 chars, one line, no padding
```

`main.py` holds all 12 contenders and the gate from the bounty rules; `--self-test` verifies every contender is gate-clean, all 12 are distinct, the picked line sits inside the 62–140 byte winning size band, is spoken in the elevator's first-person voice, carries its own "out of order" sign as its concrete marker, avoids the field entry's angle and the crowded tropes, and round-trips byte for byte.
